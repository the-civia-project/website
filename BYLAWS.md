# Bylaws | CIVIA FOUNDATION

> **DRAFT**: This document is a work in progress.

_As adopted by the Foundation Board_

\[DATE\]

These Bylaws govern the operations, structure, and mission of the Civia
Foundation (the "Foundation"), a nonprofit organization dedicated to
enabling privacy-respecting, identity-verified social networking. The
Foundation operates in accordance with applicable EU law, including
Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 as amended
by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 (eIDAS 2.0), the principles embodied in the
EU Digital Identity (EUDI) Wallet framework, and, with respect to the
protection of minors, Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (Digital Services Act).

**1. Mission**

The mission of the Civia Foundation is to build, operate, and steward an
open, trustworthy social networking platform that balances authenticated
identity with robust privacy protections. Inspired by the models of the
Signal Foundation and Proton Foundation, Civia is committed to operating
in the public interest, without profit motive, and in full alignment
with the rights of individuals in the digital space.

The Foundation pursues this mission through the following core
objectives:

- Developing and maintaining the Civia application, a social
  networking platform that verifies user identity using EUDI
  Wallet-compliant credentials under the eIDAS 2.0 framework, while
  upholding data minimization and anonymization principles.

- Promoting digital trust by ensuring all participants are real,
  accountable individuals, thereby reducing disinformation, bot
  activity, and coordinated inauthentic behavior.

- Protecting user privacy as a foundational design requirement,
  ensuring that identity verification does not equate to surveillance,
  data commodification, or unnecessary disclosure of personal
  information.

- Operating a platform permanently free from advertising and from the
  commercial exploitation of user data in any form.

- Providing a safe, chronological, and non-manipulative experience for
  all users, with algorithmic content recommendation available only as
  a strictly opt-in feature for verified adult users.

- Supporting open-source development, independent security audits, and
  transparent governance.

- Engaging in education, policy advocacy, and research in the fields
  of digital identity, privacy-preserving technologies, and
  responsible social media.

**2. Defined Terms**

For the purposes of these Bylaws, the following terms shall have the
meanings ascribed to them below:

\(a\) "Application" or "Civia" means the social networking platform
developed, operated, and maintained under the stewardship of the
Foundation.

\(b\) "Algorithmic Recommendation" means any automated system that
selects, ranks, prioritises, or otherwise curates content, accounts, or
information presented to a user based on profiling, inferred
preferences, engagement signals, or behavioural data, as distinct from a
simple reverse-chronological feed of content from accounts a user has
explicitly chosen to follow.

\(c\) "Board" means the Board of Directors of the Foundation as
constituted from time to time.

\(d\) "Data Minimization" means the principle, as set out in Article
5(1)(c) GDPR, that personal data shall be adequate, relevant, and
limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which it is
processed.

\(e\) "EUDI Wallet" means a European Digital Identity Wallet as defined
under Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 amending Regulation (EU) No 910/2014
(eIDAS 2.0), enabling natural persons to store, manage, and present
identity credentials in a privacy-preserving manner.

\(f\) "eIDAS 2.0" means Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 of the European
Parliament and of the Council, establishing the framework for a European
Digital Identity.

\(g\) "Explicit Opt-In" means a freely given, specific, informed, and
unambiguous affirmative act by a user to activate a feature or
processing activity, consistent with the consent standard of Article
4(11) and Article 7 GDPR. Pre-ticked boxes, default-on settings, and
bundled consents shall not constitute Explicit Opt-In.

\(h\) "GDPR" means Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament
and of the Council on the protection of natural persons with regard to
the processing of personal data.

\(i\) "Identity Verification" means the process by which a user's
real-world identity is confirmed through presentation of an EUDI Wallet
credential, in a manner that satisfies authentication without
unnecessary disclosure of personal data.

\(j\) "Minor" means any natural person who has not attained the age of
eighteen (18) years, as verified through the Identity Verification
process.

\(k\) "Privacy-by-Design" means the approach of embedding data
protection and privacy principles into the design of systems and
processes from inception, consistent with Article 25 GDPR.

\(l\) "Pseudomization" means the processing of personal data in such a
manner that it can no longer be attributed to a specific individual
without the use of additional information, consistent with Article 4(5)
GDPR.

\(m\) "Selective Disclosure" means the capability, enabled by EUDI
Wallet credential technologies, for a user to reveal only the minimum
necessary attributes from their identity credential --- for example,
proving legal age without revealing date of birth.

\(n\) "Targeted Marketing" means the use of personal data, behavioural
data, inferred interests, or audience profiling to select or deliver
commercial communications to specific individuals or groups, whether by
the Foundation or by any third party to whom data has been transferred.

\(o\) "Zero-Knowledge Proof" or "ZKP" means a cryptographic method by
which one party can prove to another that a given statement is true,
without conveying any additional information.

**3. Legal Structure and Seat**

The Civia Foundation is incorporated as a nonprofit foundation under
applicable law. Its registered seat and principal place of operations
shall be determined by the Board and recorded in the Foundation's
registration documents. The Foundation shall operate on a not-for-profit
basis; no part of its net earnings shall inure to the benefit of any
private individual.

The Foundation may establish offices, subsidiaries, or representative
bodies in other jurisdictions as necessary to fulfill its mission,
subject to Board approval and compliance with applicable law in each
relevant jurisdiction.

**4. Core Privacy and Identity Principles**

The Foundation's operations and the Civia platform shall be governed at
all times by the following binding principles:

**4.1 Identity Verification via EUDI Wallet**

User accounts on the Civia platform shall require identity verification
through a valid EUDI Wallet credential compliant with eIDAS 2.0. This
requirement ensures that all participants are real, natural persons. The
Foundation shall not permit bot accounts, automated agents, or
unverified entities to hold user accounts.

The Foundation shall implement Identity Verification in a manner that:

- Relies on Selective Disclosure so that users need only present the
  minimum required credential attributes (e.g., proof of being a legal
  adult in a jurisdiction) without exposing full identity documents;

- Employs Zero-Knowledge Proofs or equivalent cryptographic techniques
  wherever technically feasible to avoid unnecessary data transfer;

- Does not store raw identity credentials or full personal identifiers
  beyond what is strictly necessary for verification purposes; and

- Complies fully with Articles 5, 25, and 32 GDPR, and with the
  technical specifications issued under eIDAS 2.0 and the EUDI Wallet
  framework.

**4.2 Data Minimization and Purpose Limitation**

The Foundation shall collect and process only such personal data as is
strictly necessary to deliver the services described in its mission.
Data shall be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes
and shall not be processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes,
consistent with Article 5(1)(b) GDPR.

In particular:

- The Foundation shall not collect, sell, license, or otherwise
  commercialize user personal data for advertising, Targeted
  Marketing, or any third-party commercial purpose;

- User content and interaction data shall be processed solely for the
  purpose of delivering platform functionality and, where applicable,
  safety and trust enforcement; and

- The Foundation shall publish a clear and accessible Privacy Notice
  detailing all categories of data processed, legal bases, retention
  periods, and user rights.

**4.3 Pseudonymization and On-Platform Anonymity**

While identity verification is required at registration, users shall
have the right to engage on the Civia platform under a chosen pseudonym
or handle. The link between a user's verified identity and their
on-platform pseudonym shall be protected through strong technical and
organizational measures, including:

- Cryptographic separation between identity verification records and
  social activity data;

- Access controls ensuring that the mapping between a verified
  identity and a user account is accessible only under lawful process
  or explicit user request; and

- Prohibition on the Foundation proactively linking or publishing a
  user's real identity alongside their platform activity, except where
  required by a valid legal obligation.

**4.4 Privacy by Design and Default**

All products, systems, and processes developed or operated by the
Foundation shall embed Privacy-by-Design from inception, consistent with
Article 25 GDPR. Privacy-protective settings shall be the default. The
Foundation shall conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) as
required by Article 35 GDPR for high-risk processing activities.

**4.5 Security and Encryption**

The Foundation shall apply state-of-the-art encryption and security
practices to all data in transit and at rest. The Foundation shall
conduct regular independent security audits, disclose material
vulnerabilities responsibly, and maintain a public bug bounty program.
End-to-end encryption shall be applied to private communications where
technically feasible.

**4.6 No Surveillance Business Model**

The Foundation shall not operate a surveillance-based business model.
The Civia platform shall not serve behavioral advertising. The
Foundation's primary revenue model shall be based on voluntary user
contributions, grants, and mission-aligned partnerships, consistent with
the models of the Signal Foundation and Proton Foundation. Any
partnership or commercial arrangement must be reviewed by the Board to
confirm it does not compromise user privacy or the Foundation's
independence.

**5. Prohibition on Advertising and Commercialization of User Data**

**5.1 Absolute Prohibition on Advertising**

The Civia platform shall be permanently and unconditionally free from
advertising of any kind. This prohibition is absolute and shall not be
subject to waiver, suspension, or exception by the Board, the Executive
Director, or any other person or body. For the avoidance of doubt, this
prohibition includes:

- Display advertising, banner advertising, sponsored posts, promoted
  content, and native advertising;

- Audio and video advertising, including pre-roll, mid-roll, and
  interstitial formats;

- Paid placement of any content, account, or product in feeds, search
  results, or recommendation surfaces;

- Affiliate marketing arrangements that result in content being
  presented or ranked on the basis of commercial consideration; and

- Any other commercial arrangement under which a third party pays for
  exposure to Civia users or for preferential treatment of content.

Any amendment to this Section 5.1 shall be null and void, regardless of
the procedure by which it is purportedly adopted. The prohibition on
advertising is a constitutive element of the Foundation's mission and
shall survive any amendment to these Bylaws.

**5.2 Prohibition on Targeted Marketing Use of Data**

Neither the Foundation nor any successor, assignee, contractor, or third
party acting on behalf of the Foundation shall use, sell, license,
transfer, share, or otherwise make available any user personal data,
behavioural data, interaction data, or inferred user attributes for the
purpose of Targeted Marketing, regardless of whether such Targeted
Marketing is conducted on the Civia platform or elsewhere.

This prohibition applies to:

- Direct sale or licensing of user data to advertising networks, data
  brokers, or marketing platforms;

- Transfer of data to third parties through API access, data
  partnerships, or platform integrations where the Foundation has
  reason to believe such data will be used for Targeted Marketing;

- The construction or maintenance of advertising audience profiles,
  interest graphs, or behavioural models derived from user activity on
  Civia; and

- Any arrangement, however structured, that results in user data being
  used to select or deliver commercial messages to individuals.

The Foundation shall include contractual prohibitions on Targeted
Marketing use in all agreements with service providers, contractors, and
partners who have access to user data, and shall monitor compliance with
such prohibitions.

**5.3 Permitted Commercial Relationships**

Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Foundation may enter into commercial
relationships that do not involve advertising or the commercialization
of user data, including:

- Mission-aligned sponsorships or institutional partnerships that are
  disclosed transparently to users and that do not grant any sponsor
  access to user data or influence over content presentation;

- Revenue from optional user subscriptions that provide enhanced
  features, provided that no features essential to basic privacy or
  safety are withheld from non-paying users; and

- Grants and contracts from public bodies, research institutions, or
  philanthropic organizations for specific mission-aligned activities.

All commercial relationships shall be reviewed and approved by the Board
and disclosed in the Foundation's annual report.

**6. Algorithmic Recommendations and Protection of Minors**

**6.1 Default Feed: Chronological and Non-Algorithmic**

The default content experience on the Civia platform shall be a
chronological feed of content from accounts a user has explicitly chosen
to follow. No Algorithmic Recommendation system shall be applied to any
user's feed, discovery surface, or notification stream by default. The
Foundation commits to a non-manipulative user experience as a core
design principle.

**6.2 Absolute Prohibition on Algorithmic Recommendations for Minors**

Algorithmic Recommendations shall under no circumstances be made
available to any user who is a Minor. This prohibition is absolute and
applies regardless of parental or guardian consent. The Foundation shall
implement technical measures to enforce this prohibition, including:

- Age verification through the Identity Verification process, using
  Selective Disclosure or Zero-Knowledge Proof of age to confirm that
  a user has attained eighteen (18) years of age without revealing
  their precise date of birth;

- Permanent suppression of all Algorithmic Recommendation features in
  the platform interface for accounts verified as belonging to a
  Minor;

- Prohibition on any secondary profiling or engagement-optimisation
  processing of a Minor's activity data, whether or not such
  processing would result in visible recommendations; and

- Regular technical audits to verify that no Algorithmic
  Recommendation pathways are accessible by Minor accounts, whether
  through standard interfaces or through API or developer access.

This prohibition is consistent with and supplements the obligations
arising under Article 28 of Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (Digital Services
Act) and, where applicable, national legislation implementing additional
protections for minors online.

**6.3 Opt-In Algorithmic Recommendations for Adult Users**

For users who have been verified as having attained the age of eighteen
(18) years, the Foundation may offer Algorithmic Recommendations as an
optional feature, subject to the following conditions:

- Activation of Algorithmic Recommendations shall require Explicit
  Opt-In by the user. The feature shall be presented in a neutral
  manner, without dark patterns, pre-selected defaults, or repeated
  prompting designed to encourage uptake;

- At the point of opt-in, the Foundation shall provide clear and
  plain-language disclosure of: (i) what data is used to generate
  recommendations; (ii) the purpose and logic of the recommendation
  system; (iii) the absence of any commercial or advertising influence
  on recommendations; and (iv) how the user can deactivate the feature
  at any time;

- The recommendation system shall not incorporate
  engagement-maximisation objectives that prioritise emotionally
  provocative, divisive, or addictive content. The Foundation shall
  publish the design principles and objectives of any recommendation
  system it operates;

- Users who have opted in may withdraw that consent at any time with
  immediate effect, reverting to a chronological feed. Withdrawal of
  consent shall be as easy as the original opt-in; and

- The Foundation shall conduct and publish periodic impact assessments
  of its recommendation system to evaluate effects on user wellbeing,
  information diversity, and compliance with applicable law.

**6.4 No Profiling of Minors**

The Foundation shall not carry out profiling of Minor users, as defined
in Article 4(4) GDPR, for any purpose. Data generated by Minor users'
activity on the platform shall be used solely for the purposes of
delivering the core service (content display, communication, safety
enforcement) and shall not be used to infer interests, characteristics,
or preferences, or to train machine learning models in a manner that
creates individual profiles of Minor users.

**6.5 Additional Safeguards for Minors**

In addition to the restrictions on Algorithmic Recommendations and
profiling, the Foundation shall implement the following protections for
Minor users, consistent with the best interests of the child as a
primary consideration:

- Privacy settings for Minor accounts shall default to the most
  restrictive available options;

- Direct messaging from non-connected users to Minor accounts shall be
  disabled by default;

- The Foundation shall not send marketing communications of any kind
  to Minor users; and

- The Foundation shall designate a Child Safety Officer or equivalent
  responsible person to oversee the implementation of minor protection
  policies and to receive reports of concerns regarding Minor users.

**7. Membership**

The Foundation shall have no membership class with voting rights in the
ordinary sense of a membership organization. Governance authority is
vested exclusively in the Board of Directors, subject to the oversight
provisions set forth herein.

The Foundation may, at the Board's discretion, establish an Advisory
Council or Community Council composed of representatives of civil
society, the academic community, privacy advocates, and technical
experts. The Advisory Council shall have no binding governance authority
but shall provide recommendations to the Board on platform policies,
product roadmap, and privacy standards.

**8. Board of Directors**

**8.1 Composition**

The Foundation shall be governed by a Board of Directors consisting of
no fewer than three (3) and no more than nine (9) Directors. A majority
of Directors shall at all times be independent of any commercial entity
with a material financial interest in the Civia platform. No Director
shall hold a position that creates a conflict of interest with the
Foundation's mission, as determined by the Board.

**8.2 Powers and Duties**

The Board shall be responsible for:

- Determining and overseeing the strategic direction of the Foundation
  and the Civia platform;

- Approving the annual budget, financial statements, and major
  expenditures;

- Appointing and supervising the Executive Director and senior
  leadership;

- Ensuring the Foundation's compliance with applicable law, including
  GDPR, eIDAS 2.0, the Digital Services Act, and applicable nonprofit
  regulations;

- Reviewing and approving any material changes to the Foundation's
  privacy policy, data practices, or identity verification
  architecture;

- Overseeing compliance with the advertising prohibition in Section 5
  and the minor protection obligations in Section 6; and

- Safeguarding the Foundation's mission against commercial or
  political capture.

**8.3 Term and Appointment**

Directors shall serve terms of three (3) years and may be re-appointed
for a maximum of two (2) consecutive terms. Directors shall be appointed
by a majority vote of the existing Board. The Board shall publish the
criteria and process for Director appointments and shall seek candidates
with demonstrated expertise in privacy law, cryptography, civil
liberties, nonprofit governance, or related fields.

**8.4 Meetings and Quorum**

The Board shall meet at least four (4) times per year. A quorum shall
consist of a majority of Directors then in office. Decisions shall be
made by a simple majority of Directors present and voting, except where
these Bylaws specify a higher threshold. Board meetings may be held in
person or by secure remote means. Minutes of all meetings shall be
recorded and retained.

**8.5 Conflicts of Interest**

Directors shall promptly disclose any actual or potential conflict of
interest to the Board. A Director with a conflict of interest shall
recuse themselves from discussion and voting on the relevant matter. The
Board shall maintain a Conflicts of Interest Policy and require annual
disclosures from all Directors and senior staff.

**8.6 Emergency Actions**

In the event of an urgent security threat, material data breach, or
other emergency requiring immediate action, the Executive Director, in
consultation with no fewer than two (2) Directors, may authorize
emergency operational measures. Such measures shall be reported to the
full Board at the earliest opportunity. Emergency actions shall not
override the Foundation's core privacy principles as set out in Section
4, the advertising prohibition in Section 5, or the minor protection
provisions in Section 6.

**9. Executive Director and Staff**

The Board shall appoint an Executive Director to manage the day-to-day
operations of the Foundation. The Executive Director shall be
accountable to the Board and shall implement Board decisions, lead
organizational staff, manage financial operations within Board-approved
budgets, and represent the Foundation externally.

The Foundation shall appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO) as required
under Article 37 GDPR. The DPO shall operate independently and report
directly to the Board on data protection matters. The DPO shall not
receive instructions from the Executive Director or other staff
regarding the exercise of DPO functions.

The Foundation shall designate a Child Safety Officer responsible for
overseeing compliance with Section 6 of these Bylaws, liaising with
relevant authorities on matters concerning Minor users, and receiving
and responding to safeguarding concerns.

**10. Funding and Financial Governance**

The Foundation shall be funded through:

- Voluntary user donations and subscriptions that do not grant access
  to user data or compromise platform neutrality;

- Grants from public institutions, foundations, and mission-aligned
  philanthropic organizations;

- Revenue from privacy-respecting ancillary services, where approved
  by the Board; and

- Any other sources approved by the Board as consistent with the
  Foundation's mission and independence.

The Foundation shall maintain full financial transparency. Annual
financial statements, audited by an independent external auditor, shall
be published publicly. No Director, officer, or employee shall derive
private benefit from Foundation assets in excess of reasonable
compensation for services rendered.

**11. Transparency and Accountability**

The Foundation is committed to radical transparency in its governance
and operations. The Foundation shall publish:

- Annual reports detailing mission activities, financial accounts,
  governance decisions, and privacy impact assessments;

- A Transparency Report at least annually, disclosing the number and
  nature of legal requests for user data received and the Foundation's
  responses thereto, to the extent permitted by law;

- An annual compliance report addressing the Foundation's adherence to
  Sections 5 and 6 of these Bylaws, including confirmation that no
  advertising revenue was received and that no data was used for
  Targeted Marketing purposes;

- Summaries of Board meeting decisions (excluding matters subject to
  legal privilege or security sensitivity);

- All significant changes to the platform's privacy architecture,
  identity verification procedures, or data practices, with advance
  notice to users where reasonably practicable; and

- The Foundation's open-source code repositories and security audit
  reports.

**12. Amendments to Bylaws**

These Bylaws may be amended by a two-thirds (2/3) majority vote of the
full Board of Directors at a duly convened meeting, provided that:

- Written notice of the proposed amendment, including the text of the
  proposed change and the rationale, is provided to all Directors no
  fewer than thirty (30) days prior to the vote;

- Any amendment that materially alters the core privacy and identity
  principles set out in Section 4, the advertising and data
  commercialization prohibitions in Section 5, or the minor protection
  provisions in Section 6 shall additionally require review and
  written opinion from the Foundation's DPO; and

- Amendments shall be published publicly and communicated to the user
  community within thirty (30) days of adoption.

For the avoidance of doubt, Section 5.1 (Absolute Prohibition on
Advertising) may not be amended, repealed, or suspended by any vote of
the Board or any other body. This restriction is a foundational term of
the Foundation's constitution.

**13. Dissolution**

In the event of dissolution of the Foundation, after satisfaction of all
outstanding liabilities and obligations, the remaining assets shall be
transferred to one or more nonprofit organizations with missions
substantially similar to that of the Foundation, as determined by the
Board. No assets shall be distributed to Directors, officers, or
employees upon dissolution, except for compensation of services already
rendered.

Upon dissolution, the Foundation shall securely delete or anonymize all
user personal data in its custody, in accordance with GDPR obligations
and user rights, unless legal obligations require otherwise.

**14. Governing Law and Dispute Resolution**

These Bylaws shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the
law of the jurisdiction in which the Foundation is incorporated, as
determined by the Board and recorded in the Foundation's registration
documents.

Any dispute arising out of or in connection with these Bylaws that
cannot be resolved internally shall be submitted to mediation. If
mediation is unsuccessful within sixty (60) days, the dispute shall be
resolved by binding arbitration in accordance with the arbitration rules
of a recognized international arbitration institution, to be designated
by the Board. The language of proceedings shall be English.
